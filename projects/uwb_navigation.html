Collaborative navigation

Intervehicle UWB ranging for GNSS-degraded navigation.

A research project with Joey Cahill at Virginia Tech focused on direct vehicle-to-vehicle ranging constraints, UWB error characterization, and future Kalman-filter integration.

Problem

GNSS spoofing and interference can make a vehicle converge to a confident but false navigation state. The research question is whether commercial UWB hardware can provide direct cross-links between vehicles so relative range and heading constraints remain available when satellite navigation degrades.

My Contribution

  • Configured DWM1001C anchor/tag networks and collected static, vehicle, and calibration ranging data.
  • Built MATLAB logging and parsing tools for DWM1001 location/ranging payloads, including distance-only packet semantics.
  • Analyzed bias, RMSE, spike rate, outlier windows, and session-specific shifts across multiple geometry and distance cases.
  • Worked toward estimator-ready bias, noise, and outlier models so UWB can be incorporated into a Kalman-filter navigation framework.

Measured Results

Poster results show practical UWB operation extended from about 31 m to 110 m, with a scheduled ranging framework. A piecewise correction reduced cross-validation MAE from 0.110 m to 0.058 m. A later one-to-one calibration workflow selected a second-order runtime correction and improved CV RMSE by 36.71% over a linear baseline.

Static one-to-one analysis covered four runs with 1,779-1,797 samples per run. RMSE ranged from roughly 0.063 m to 0.113 m, and the 2.0 m tests exposed a session-specific bias sign flip, which is exactly the kind of systematic behavior that has to be modeled before feeding ranges into an estimator.

Next Validation Step

The next technical step is to replicate the simulated multi-transceiver configuration on vehicle roof mounts, collect synchronized range streams, and validate the measurement model against inertial and vehicle-motion dynamics.
